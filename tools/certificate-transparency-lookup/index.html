What are Certificate Transparency logs?

Certificate Transparency (CT) is a public, append-only record of every TLS/SSL certificate issued by a participating certificate authority. Whenever a CA issues a certificate, it must log it in public CT logs — otherwise major browsers won't trust it.

For bug bounty hunters and security researchers, CT logs are one of the highest-signal passive recon sources: they reveal hidden subdomains (every SAN in a cert is a hostname), internal naming patterns (dev-, staging-, vpn.), and orphaned infrastructure — certs issued long ago for hosts that may no longer exist.

Why use a CT lookup?

  • Subdomain discovery: any hostname a company ever got a certificate for shows up here — even ones never linked from anywhere.
  • Finding expired infrastructure: an expired cert for a still-live hostname is a classic takeover / forgotten-service lead.
  • Issuer hygiene: certificates from unusual CAs can flag mis-issued or test certificates.

How this tool works

This page queries the public crt.sh JSON API from your browser and renders the results locally. No data is stored, no account is needed, and the domain you search never touches our servers. crt.sh can be slow for very large domains — if a search times out, try a narrower domain (e.g. app.example.com instead of com-level TLDs).
